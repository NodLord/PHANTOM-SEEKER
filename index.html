00 // MISSION PHILOSOPHY

REMEMBER WHAT DIED.
MEASURE WHAT REMAINS.

Phantom Seeker is not a race toward distance. It is an expedition into memory.

We will follow human conspiracies into the Formidine Rift, stop beside the memorial of an animal loved enough to be remembered among the stars, stand before abandoned settlements and the carcass of the Zurara, then leave human history behind and enter the territory of another extinct civilization.

The second half of the voyage is not pilgrimage alone. It is field archaeology.

Around HEN 2-333 and through Norma Arm, we will use known Guardian ruins and Brain Tree populations as reference data, then push outward into poorly explored systems in search of structures, biological traces and spatial patterns that have escaped previous surveys.

Every waypoint therefore has two purposes: remember what died there, and measure what remains.

PROLOGUE

TEORGE

THE RAGAZZA TRANSMISSIONS

Phantom Seeker begins not with a jump into wilderness, but with a warning.

Hidden throughout the Teorge system are seven Ragazza Listening Posts, carrying the recordings that expose the hidden machinery behind the events leading to Project Dynasty.

Commanders should locate and scan all seven before departure. They can be found around Teorge 1 C, 3 C, 3 E, 4 A, 4 B, 5 D and 5 E.

Together, their transmissions reveal the existence of The Club, contingency planning for humanity’s survival, the Exodus conspiracy, the Dynasty expeditions and the terrible logic behind sending crews into the distant reaches of the galaxy.

This is our briefing room.

The logs should be read as one continuous story. Those unfamiliar with the events should avoid spoilers and recover the transmissions in sequence.

Names, dates, motives and references to Dynasty should be noted.

When the seventh voice falls silent, USS Frank’s Pharmacy departs.
The dead have given us the route.

CHAPTER IGHOSTS OF FORMIDINE

Chapter I follows human ghosts.

It is an investigation into Project Dynasty, Cmdr Salomé, the abandoned Eafots settlements and the Zurara. The purpose is not merely to revisit famous locations, but to reconstruct the operation as a sequence of evidence.

We move from memory, to suspicion, to proof, and finally to the grave.

WP1
THE GOOD BOY

Heart Sector IR-V b2-0

Memorial for Fluk

Before we begin following men and women who were sent into the dark to die, we stop for someone who never knew politics, conspiracies or extinction.

Near body A 1 is the Memorial for Fluk, a Listening Post dedicated to a dead dog.

It has no archaeological value. No Guardian signature. No secret code.

That is precisely why we stop.

Phantom Seeker is an expedition about what remains after life is gone. Human, alien, animal, it makes no difference to the dark.

Commanders should locate the memorial, scan it, read the message and pay their respects. Farsight Expedition Base may be used for repairs or rendezvous, but the memorial is the reason this system belongs on our route.

No measurements are required here.

Just remembrance.
Then we continue.

WP2
SALOMÉ’S QUESTION

Eafots RX-T d3-3

Salomé’s World

Here the route changes from memorial to investigation.

Cmdr Salomé passed through this system during her search for the truth surrounding the Formidine Rift. Particular attention became attached to its Ammonia Worlds. She was killed trying to expose the Exodus conspiracy on 29th April 3303.

Why these worlds?

We do not know.

That uncertainty is enough.

Commanders should perform a complete FSS survey, inspect the Ammonia Worlds, record their moons and orbital architecture, DSS scientifically interesting bodies and examine neighboring systems for unusual exploration gaps.

This waypoint introduces one of Phantom Seeker’s central principles:
a question does not become unimportant merely because nobody has answered it yet.

WP3
ALPHA

Eafots EU-R c4-1

Dynasty Settlement Alpha

The first grave with walls.

Alpha begins the archaeological core of Chapter I.

Commanders should land, inspect the settlement thoroughly, recover every available log and photograph the site before leaving. Details concerning navigation, morale, propulsion, unexplained phenomena and mission orders should be recorded.

Do not listen to one log and jump away.

These settlements must be treated as four fragments of a single dataset.

Alpha is fragment one.

WP4
BETA

Eafots LZ-H b10-0

Dynasty Settlement Beta

Another camp. Another crew that did not go home.

At Beta, the expedition repeats the Alpha protocol: complete recovery of logs, surface inspection and documentation.

Special attention should be paid to drive failures, emergency events and descriptions of hyperspace or anomalous signals.

One doomed crew can be dismissed as tragedy.

Four crews allow comparison.

The ghosts become statistics.

WP5
GAMMA

Eafots RA-G b11-0

Dynasty Settlement Gamma

Gamma is our third independent witness.

Commanders should again document the complete site, then compare its chronology with Alpha and Beta.

By this point we should begin asking whether failures and psychological deterioration occurred randomly or whether the same kinds of events repeated across multiple expedition teams.

That distinction matters.

Coincidence is cheap.

Patterns are expensive.

WP6
DELTA

Eafots GL-Y e2

Dynasty Settlement Delta

The last of the four Formidine Dynasty settlements.

Delta completes the terrestrial evidence.

All logs should be recovered before the fleet moves on. Once Alpha through Delta have been visited, commanders are encouraged to compare their notes while still in the region.

What repeated?

What did not?

What did the crews know?

What were they never told?

After Delta, only one witness remains.
It cannot speak except through wreckage.

WP7
THE TOMB

Syreadiae JX-F c0

The Zurara

The Zurara is where Chapter I reaches its end.

Project Dynasty sent men and women beyond civilization to search for places humanity might one day flee to.

The ship survived the journey.

Its crew did not survive the secret.

USS Frank’s Pharmacy should remain in the system long enough for every participating commander to visit the megaship, recover the logs and conduct a full survey of the surrounding system.

Afterward, independent exploration flights should extend into the nearby region rather than immediately departing. Earth-like Worlds, Ammonia Worlds, Water Worlds and unusually incomplete systems deserve particular attention.

The Zurara is not simply a destination.

It is the point where the human ghosts stop leading us.

From here onward, we choose the road ourselves.

WP8
THE CROSSING

Bubble Sector PD-S b4-4

Bubble Nebula

Bubble Nebula is our transition between the two halves of Phantom Seeker.

We leave Formidine behind and turn toward Norma.

The carrier may remain here briefly while commanders perform local exploration, help refuel Tritium, have a drink at the Pharmacy’s bar to lift the mood after Chapter I, or simply enjoy the truly unique shape and color of the nebula. Tip: commanders should jump outside the nebula to experience the full scenery.

There is no major Dynasty investigation required at this waypoint.

Its purpose is geographical and psychological.

Behind us lie human graves.

Ahead lie the remains of something much older.

The first chapter ends beneath the light of the nebula.

CHAPTER IITHE DEAD OF ANOTHER AGE

Humanity has names for the people lost in Project Dynasty.

The Guardians left us fewer names.

Their cities remain, their machines still answer certain commands, their biological traces persist on distant worlds, but the individuals are gone.

Billions of lives reduced to geometry.

Chapter II attempts to read that geometry.

Our method is simple: study a region where Guardian occupation is known, establish its spatial and environmental signature, then carry that model into less explored regions of Norma Arm.

We are no longer following a written story.

We are trying to reconstruct one from ruins.

WP9
THE CLOSED DOOR

Graea Hypue QL-V b19-15

HEN 2-333 Survey Base

USS Frank’s Pharmacy establishes the principal archaeological base here.

The system lies immediately outside the HEN 2-333 permit-locked region and contains known Guardian ruins. It therefore gives Phantom Seeker a perfect calibration point: known archaeology beside an inaccessible centre.

This is where Phantom Grid becomes an operational tool rather than a map.

Commanders should first visit the known ruins and document their planetary environments. After calibration, survey flights should radiate outward through the HEN region, beginning near the approximate permit boundary and expanding through the 150, 300 and 400 light-year shells.

Brain Tree systems, known Guardian sites, virgin systems and deliberate negative surveys should all be recorded separately.

And the primary archaeological ambition must remain explicit:

we are here to discover Guardian ruins or structures that nobody has yet catalogued.

The known sites teach us where the Guardians were.

The unexplored systems may tell us where else they went.

We must never confuse:

“nothing has been reported here”

with

“nothing exists here.”

That distinction may decide whether we find anything at all.

HEN is the laboratory.

Everything after HEN is the experiment.

WP10
THE FIRST BLIND TEST

Froarks NN-R d5-520

Froarks Survey Zone

The carrier now enters Norma Arm proper.

At Froarks, we deliberately leave the comfort of a known Guardian concentration.

The objective is to test whether the signatures learned around HEN continue into a region containing large volumes of poorly explored space.

USS Frank’s Pharmacy becomes the fixed anchor while smaller ships conduct radial expeditions into nearby systems and surrounding nebular volumes.

Priority should be given to systems still marked as undiscovered, landable rocky or HMC worlds, Brain Tree-compatible environments, unusual biological signals and regions that resemble the spatial structure observed around HEN.

But commanders must also record failure.

If Froarks resembles HEN physically and produces no Guardian evidence, that matters.

Science sometimes answers with silence.

WP11
THE DEEP DARK

Iowhail TI-K d8-1514

Iowhail Survey Zone

Iowhail Nebula is where Phantom Seeker becomes a true deep-survey expedition.

The region lies within the dense galactic disk and contains enormous numbers of systems that remain untouched or incompletely documented.

Here the emphasis shifts heavily toward virgin-system discovery.

Commanders should use Phantom Link, SRV Survey or equivalent tools to identify systems absent from community coverage, enter them, perform FSS surveys and report whether the system was previously discovered.

Every virgin system found by the expedition becomes a new point on Phantom Grid.

Every fully surveyed negative becomes equally valuable.

We are mapping ignorance itself.

And somewhere inside that ignorance may be another ruin.

WP12
THE LAST SEARCH

Byua Aim LX-K d8-2459

Byua Aim Survey Zone

This is the final archaeological field camp of Phantom Seeker.

By now we should possess something we did not have when leaving Teorge:

experience.

We will have studied the HEN colony, measured Brain Tree distributions, accumulated negative surveys, crossed poorly explored sectors and tested our assumptions in multiple regions.

Byua Aim is where all of that is applied together.

No single criterion should decide where commanders search.

Targets should be chosen using the complete expedition picture: unexplored-system density, distance from known Guardian populations, Brain Tree distribution, planetary environment, stellar geography and the negative evidence collected at earlier camps.

This is not merely the last waypoint.

It is the final test of the hypothesis.

If Phantom Seeker finds an unknown Guardian site here, the expedition will have changed the archaeological map.

If we find nothing, we will still return with a much sharper picture of where the Guardians were not.

Both outcomes have value.

The dead do not owe us a discovery.

EPILOGUE

DERISO

HOME AMONG THE LIVING

The final jump is toward Deriso, in the Colonia region.

USS Frank’s Pharmacy returns carrying whatever Phantom Seeker earned: exploration data, biological records, archaeological observations, photographs, unanswered questions and perhaps a few coordinates that did not exist on our maps when we departed.

Commanders sell their data.

The fleet repairs.

Phantom Grid is updated.

The expedition report is assembled.

Only then do we decide what Phantom Seeker actually discovered.

Maybe there will be a new Guardian ruin.

Maybe a new Brain Tree province.

Maybe thousands of virgin systems.

Maybe only a better boundary around an ancient civilization.

That is enough.

Because this expedition was never merely about finding monuments.

It was about listening to what remains.

A dead dog remembered beside a distant star.

Human explorers buried by the secret they carried.

An alien civilization whose cities still glow long after its people vanished.

And the black spaces between all of them, where nobody has yet looked.

We followed their ghosts across the galaxy.

Now we bring their coordinates home.
